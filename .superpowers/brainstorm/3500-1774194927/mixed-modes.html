A Triadic core + Analogous neighbours of the dominant hue
The 3 triadic hues stay vivid. Extra slots pick analogous neighbours of the brand hue (±25°, ±45°), at varied L so they read as warm/cool variants rather than duplicates. You get a warm cluster around the brand plus cool contrasts from the triadic pair.

B Triadic core + Tints/Shades of each hue (L ladder)
Core hues stay at mid L (±0.62). Each extra slot is a tint (L+0.20, C×0.5) or shade (L−0.25, C×0.8) of the triadic hue closest in rotation. You keep the same hue spectrum but dramatically expand the lightness range — exactly like Tailwind's 50–950 scale, but just 2–3 steps per hue.

C Triadic core + Split-complement accent cluster + Neutral anchors
The 3 triadic hues fill slots 1–3. A split-complement of the brand hue generates 1–2 accent slots that feel "different but related". A near-white and near-black round out the set. This is closest to how real design systems (Radix, Material) build their palettes — structured hue theory + intentional neutrals.

My read: A gives the warmest, most cohesive feel but risks looking too monothematic. B gives the most "design system ready" tint/shade ladder. C gives the most visual surprise — the split-complement accents feel genuinely unexpected while the anchors make it immediately usable. Which direction feels closest to what you want? We can also combine (e.g. B's tint ladder + C's neutral anchors).
